Pflugerville's Real Local Airport — and Why It Isn't Apex's

Unlike most Austin-area suburbs, Pflugerville actually has a real general aviation airport immediately next door: Austin Executive Airport (KEDC), a privately owned field in Travis County, just southeast of Pflugerville proper, sitting at about 620 feet of elevation. The airport under that name opened in 2011, when a redevelopment expanded and reoriented the runway — but the site itself had already been a public-use GA airfield for roughly 40 years before that, under the name Bird's Nest Airport (FAA: 6R4). So it's more accurate to say Austin Executive Airport is a 2011 rebrand and expansion of a decades-old airfield, not a brand-new airport built from scratch. That's a genuinely useful local fact for Pflugerville pilots — and also exactly why we want to be direct about it: Apex Aviation has no base, lease, hangar, or any operating connection to KEDC. It's a privately owned airport we don't operate at, full stop.

Our own proposed location is Taylor Municipal Airport (T74), a different field entirely — publicly owned by the City of Taylor, non-towered, with one asphalt runway and a CTAF of 122.8. If the T74 proposal comes together, Pflugerville pilots would be commuting to it, not training at KEDC.

A CFI's Take: Training Near Austin's Busy Airspace

Pflugerville sits close enough to Austin-Bergstrom's Class C airspace, and to KEDC's own busy traffic pattern, that pilots training in this corridor get exposed early to the kind of radio discipline and situational awareness that's harder to practice out in open country — working an approach or departure sequence around jet traffic, reading a controller's instructions correctly the first time, and knowing when to speak up versus when to stay off the frequency. That's a genuine training advantage of this part of the Austin area, not something unique to one airport. We've written a longer guide on navigating this specific airspace — see Flying Austin's Class C Airspace: A Pilot's Guide — rather than repeat it here.
