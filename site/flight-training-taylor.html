Serving Pilots from Taylor and the Surrounding Area

Taylor is experiencing a second boom, driven by Samsung's major semiconductor investment and the professionals and families arriving with it. Aviation interest is rising alongside that growth. Taylor Municipal Airport (T74) serves local general aviation, but structured, scenario-based flight instruction has not been readily available to Taylor-area pilots without a significant commute. Apex Aviation is working to close that gap — proposing a single-aircraft, instructor-led Part 61 operation based right at T74, pending airport approval and aircraft arrangements, with a curriculum designed for pilots who want to advance efficiently without the inefficiency of long drives.

Where Would I Train?

Our preferred location is Taylor Municipal Airport (T74), a public, non-towered airport (CTAF 122.8) owned by the City of Taylor, sitting about two nautical miles west of downtown Taylor. It's a short, direct drive from Hutto and Georgetown as well — close enough that recurring weekday and weekend lessons are realistic without a long commute eating into your training time.

We haven't published an exact drive time for this route, since the final lesson location (ramp space, parking, where you'd meet your instructor) isn't settled yet. If you want to see the drive for yourself in the meantime, here's a direct link: get directions to Taylor Municipal Airport (T74) →

Important: T74 is not yet an operating Apex Aviation location. We're actively working through airport approval, ramp/parking arrangements, and aircraft logistics before we can confirm it. Everything on this page describing in-person training at T74 is a proposal we're planning toward, not a service available today.

When Can I Start?

March 2027 is our planning target for opening the proposed Taylor Municipal Airport location — it is not a confirmed launch date. Airport approval, parking, and aircraft arrangements are all still pending, and the timeline could move depending on how those pieces come together. If you join the interest list below, we'll keep you updated as each piece is confirmed, well before any public enrollment opens.

How Training Would Work

The proposed program would operate under Part 61, with Andrew as primary instructor on one dedicated aircraft — the expected aircraft is a Piper PA-28, currently under lease negotiation. For independent study, we're planning to use Sporty's as the primary ground-school course, supplemented by instructor-led ground sessions with Andrew — so you'd build your aeronautical knowledge on your own schedule and still get live, structured ground time before and after flights.

What Would It Cost?

The rates we're proposing for this location are $185/hour for the aircraft, with fuel included, and $75/hour for instruction — $260 total for an hour that bills both. These are proposed rates for planning purposes, not a locked-in price list: ground instruction, checkride fees, and other training costs are separate and will affect what your total training actually costs.

We're not publishing a guaranteed number of hours to finish your certificate or a bundled package price. Every student progresses at a different pace, and a specific hour-count promise or discounted package isn't something we can responsibly commit to before we even know our own costs at a confirmed location. For the broader picture of what Apex charges across all of its programs, see our full pricing page.

Never Flown Before? Only Done a Discovery Flight?

Most students start with the basics: how the aircraft controls work, straight-and-level flight, climbs and descents, and getting comfortable simply being in the airplane. If you've only taken a single discovery flight, that's a completely normal starting point — those first few lessons build the foundation everything else sits on. Each lesson opens with a short brief on what you're working on and closes with an honest debrief on what went well and what to revisit next time.

You don't have to wait for the T74 location to start preparing. Apex Advantage Private Instruction is live right now — 1-on-1, online, $55/hour, every certificate level — and it's a practical way to start building your aeronautical knowledge, work through the private pilot written test material, and get comfortable with the concepts before you ever need to book flight time. Can I Fit Training Around My Schedule?

Flight training works best on a recurring schedule — the same day and time slot most weeks — rather than booked one lesson at a time whenever something opens up. Recurring windows protect your spot, keep skills from going stale between lessons, and make it much easier for your instructor to plan a curriculum that actually builds week over week instead of re-covering the same ground. When you join the interest list below, tell us two times that would work for you most weeks — it helps us understand what a realistic schedule looks like for Taylor-area students before we finalize how we structure lesson availability.
